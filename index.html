★Beginner Guide — your first song, step by step

New to Harmony AI Studio? Follow this path from a voice recording to a finished, playable song. You do not need music-production experience.

1 · Start a projectGive your session a name at the top. Use Save regularly.
2 · Record your ideaTap Record, sing or hum your melody, then stop. Use headphones if possible.
3 · Check what Harmony heardReview the estimated key, BPM and voice information. Correct anything that sounds wrong.
4 · Choose your directionUse the genre, mood and Musical DNA controls to tell the producer what kind of musical world you want.
5 · Let the AI arrange itUse Auto-produce full song or AI Song Autopilot. Review the arrangement before exporting.
6 · Listen before you judgePress Play Full Mix. Test your phone speaker or headphones. If needed, use Render & Play for a rendered WAV playback path.
7 · Shape the mixCheck the vocal-first balance, stereo field, low end and overall level. Keep the lead vocal clear.
8 · Learn the partsOpen Harmony Academy to practise keyboard, guitar or bass parts. Slow the lesson down when needed.
9 · Prepare musiciansOpen Player Book for notes, sections, cues and performance information that can be handed to live musicians.
10 · Save and exportSave the project, export a portable backup, then export WAV/MIDI/Player Book files when the song is ready.
Three simple workflows
🎤 Singer / songwriter

Record → Analyse → Auto-produce → Listen → Adjust key → Render → Export.

🎹 Producer / musician

Skip recording → Set key/BPM → Build → Edit parts → Academy practice → Player Book → Export.

🎓 Student

Select a song → Open Academy → Start Guided mode → practise slowly → review your score → repeat weak sections.

?Help & troubleshooting

If something does not behave as expected, use this quick checklist before changing your project.

🔊 No sound

Tap Test speakers / headphones. Check the phone/PC volume and Bluetooth output. If live playback is blocked, use Render & play.

🎙 Mic not working

Allow microphone access for the browser. On a phone, try Record with phone recorder and import the file instead.

💾 Moving devices

Use Backup project before changing browsers or phones. Restore the JSON backup on the new device.

🎹 Learning a part

Open Harmony Academy, select the instrument and song section, start at 50% speed, then move to Developing or Independent.

Key and voice parts

The studio can distinguish soprano, mezzo-soprano, alto/contralto, tenor, baritone and bass as provisional voice-family estimates. It also reports the sung key, observed range, tessitura and notation convention. “Treble” is treated correctly as a register/clef concept, not a voice type.

AI Song Autopilot — describe the song and let the producer build it
Voice identity: when you provide your own recording, the studio can use it as a reference for range, phrasing and vocal character. A future connected singing model can render the generated performance in that authorised voice; this browser build does not pretend that a simple microphone recording can magically create a natural cloned singer.
Musical DNA — influence library & originality engine
Build a new identity. Choose musical characteristics you admire. Harmony AI Studio abstracts phrasing, harmony, groove, arrangement, instrumentation and worship dynamics; it does not copy a recording or recreate an artist's exact voice.
Production blueprint — why each sound enters

The producer uses role rules: rhythm first, low-end second, harmonic support third, colour last. It avoids filling every gap just because an instrument is available.

Parts and voice
Mixer — intelligent stereo balance

Harmony AI Studio automatically protects the lead vocal, keeps bass and kick centered, places supporting instruments across the stereo field, preserves mono compatibility, and applies gentle bus compression/limiting so no section overwhelms another.

Your own beat loops

Add royalty-free drum or beat loops. They are stretched to your tempo and play through the verses and choruses. Files stay on your device.

Player Book — exact production documentation

This is the handoff for a live band. Every generated musical role is documented as a part: what instrument to use, what it plays, where it enters, the key/octave, rhythm, dynamics, articulation and rehearsal notes. The electronic production remains the reference master.

Build the Player Book after producing the arrangement.

Live-performance rule: players should rehearse from the individual part plus the master reference. If a note is marked as an approximation, the musician should follow the groove/phrasing instruction rather than forcing an exact transcription.
